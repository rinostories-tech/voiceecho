Why it works

Style is a measurable thing.

Detectors score generated text on two axes: perplexity, how predictable each word is, and burstiness, how much that predictability swings from sentence to sentence — measured, literally, as the standard deviation of the per-sentence scores.1 AI text scores low on both.

Detectors were built on this, and they're bad at it. They've flagged the U.S. Constitution as machine-written.2 So we're not in the business of beating a checker — a score that fires on the Constitution isn't a standard worth optimising against, and the biggest ones have since moved to other methods anyway.3

But the measurement underneath is real. Stanford researchers showed that model-written text sits in a measurably different region of a language model's probability landscape than human writing does — the signal exists whether or not any product detects it reliably.4

And it's old. In 1964 two statisticians ended a 175-year argument over the disputed Federalist Papers by counting words like upon and whilst — excluding topic words entirely, using only articles, pronouns, prepositions and conjunctions.5 Function words. The ones nobody picks on purpose. Sixty years on, the finding holds: the most identifying thing about your writing is the part you aren't thinking about.

"Punchy" is a setting.
You are a distribution.

Which is why pasting a paragraph works and a tone dropdown never will.

Signal 01
Function words
The, of, but, just, actually. Unconscious, consistent across topics, and the strongest authorship marker in the literature.
Signal 02
Sentence variance
Not average length — the spread. How hard you swing between your shortest sentence and your longest.
Signal 03
Punctuation habits
Your em-dash rate, whether you use semicolons, where you put commas you don't strictly need.
Sources & further reading
  1. Chen, Kang, Zhai, Li, Guo & Liu (2023) — On the Generalization of Training-based ChatGPT Detection Methods. Sets out how perplexity and burstiness are actually computed, burstiness being the standard deviation of per-sentence perplexity. arxiv.org/abs/2310.01307
  2. Benj Edwards, Ars Technica (2023) — Why AI detectors think the US Constitution was written by AI. The false-positive problem, explained with the creator of GPTZero. arstechnica.com
  3. GPTZero (2023) — What is perplexity & burstiness for AI detection? Their own note that they moved off these metrics in autumn 2023 in favour of a deep-learning architecture. gptzero.me
  4. Mitchell, Lee, Khazatsky, Manning & Finn (2023) — DetectGPT: Zero-Shot Machine-Generated Text Detection using Probability Curvature. Model-written text occupies negative-curvature regions of the log-probability function; the underlying statistical difference is real. arxiv.org/abs/2301.11305
  5. Mosteller & Wallace (1964) — Inference and Disputed Authorship: The Federalist. The founding work of stylometry: contextual words excluded, authorship settled on function words alone. press.uchicago.edu

Questions

Good to know.

How many samples does it need?

Two or three paragraphs you actually wrote is plenty. More real "you" gets a sharper match — polished, edited pieces work better than raw notes.

Is my writing kept private?

Your voice profiles are yours. They're stored to your account so you can reuse them, and aren't shared with other users.

What counts as one rewrite?

One rewrite of one draft counts as one. Re-running the same draft in a different voice counts as another. Your monthly allowance resets at the start of each billing month.

Can I keep separate voices?

Yes — that's the point. Keep one for your personal brand, one per client, one for the company newsletter, and switch between them.

How is this different from ChatGPT?

ChatGPT writes well, but in its voice — and you rebuild the instructions every session. EchoWrite measures how you write, saves that as a reusable profile, and applies it to any draft in one click. See the comparison above.
